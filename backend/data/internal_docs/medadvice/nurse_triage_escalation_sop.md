# Nurse Triage Escalation SOP

Scope: this standard operating procedure covers how the nurse triage line and the virtual assistant escalate an incoming symptom report. It applies to every shift, including the after-hours answering service, and the clinical quality committee reviews it each quarter.

Tier 1 (call 911 now): chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, trouble breathing, signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech), severe bleeding, fainting, or thoughts of self-harm. The triage nurse stays on the line until emergency services are confirmed and documents the time of the 911 handoff.

Tier 2 (same-day visit): an adult fever above 103°F or any fever in an infant under three months, a sore throat with drooling or trouble swallowing, symptoms that have worsened after 48 hours of home care, or a new rash with fever. The nurse books the earliest same-day slot, or directs the caller to urgent care when none is open.

Tier 3 (home care with follow-up): mild cold symptoms, a sore throat without red flags, minor sprains and mild stomach upset. The nurse gives the standard home-care guidance, confirms the caller knows when to call back, and schedules a follow-up call within 72 hours.

Documentation: every triage contact is logged in the encounter record within 30 minutes, with the tier assigned, the red-flag questions asked and the disposition. A Tier 1 or Tier 2 call that could not be completed is reported to the charge nurse before the end of the shift.

Escalation contacts: the charge nurse is paged for any caller who refuses a Tier 1 disposition. The on-call physician is paged for medication questions the nurse cannot answer from the approved OTC guidance list.
